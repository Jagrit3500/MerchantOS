MERCHANT RECOVERY PLAYBOOK - EVIDENCE FIRST

SOURCES: RBI/DPSS/2025-26/141, provider public documentation, merchant agreement, and case records
LAST VERIFIED: 12 September 2026

STEP 1 - PRESERVE THE RECORD

Capture the exact dashboard status, affected amount, transaction and settlement IDs, dates, and screenshots. Save every email and support response. Export the provider report and obtain the matching bank statement.

STEP 2 - CHECK THE CONTRACTUAL BASELINE

Confirm the settlement schedule and pricing displayed for the account. Under paragraph 16 and Table 1 of RBI/DPSS/2025-26/141, the PA-merchant agreement should fairly and transparently state settlement timing. A public default schedule may differ from the schedule assigned to a particular account.

STEP 3 - SUBMIT A PRECISE SUPPORT REQUEST

List each affected transaction. Ask for the reason, remediation items, policy or agreement clause, expected review date, and reconciliation statement. Attach only relevant evidence and do not include passwords, OTPs, or unnecessary customer personal data.

UNUSUAL VOLUME OR DISPUTE SIGNALS

A reported transaction-volume change or chargeback does not by itself establish why an account was restricted. Ask the provider whether the events are related and which provider or card-network rule applies. Preserve affected transaction and order IDs, dispute notices, invoices, fulfilment or service-completion records, customer communications, refunds, and a factual explanation of unusual business activity where relevant.

STEP 4 - COMPLETE OR CLARIFY KYC

Use the provider's current business-type checklist. Paragraph 13 of RBI/DPSS/2025-26/141 requires merchant due diligence under the RBI KYC Direction and describes CKYCR retrieval. It also provides a simplified alternative process for eligible merchants within stated turnover limits. It does not itself provide a GST checklist for each merchant type.

If a requested document is unavailable, ask whether another accepted document can satisfy the same check. Do not state that the provider is legally barred from requesting GST unless a qualified adviser has verified that conclusion for the specific facts and applicable law.

STEP 5 - USE THE PUBLISHED ESCALATION MATRIX

Paragraph 8 requires the payment aggregator to appoint an officer for merchant issues and publish an escalation matrix. Escalate with the original ticket, chronology, amount, requested outcome, and attachments. Use the provider's published timing rather than an invented universal deadline.

STEP 6 - CHECK EXTERNAL REMEDIES

The Reserve Bank - Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2021 is a cost-free route for maintainable complaints against covered regulated entities. A complainant generally must first complain in writing to the regulated entity and may approach the Ombudsman after rejection or if no reply is received within one month. Coverage, exclusions, limitation periods, and complainant status must be checked for the specific case at https://cms.rbi.org.in.

Do not state that days elapsed from the underlying issue alone establish Ombudsman eligibility. Do not promise fund release, compensation, a penalty, or a decision deadline.

SAFE REQUEST LANGUAGE

"Please provide the specific reason for the restriction, the remediation steps, the applicable merchant-policy or agreement clause, and the expected review or settlement date. Please also provide the merchant-grievance contact and escalation matrix referenced in paragraph 8 of RBI/DPSS/2025-26/141."

FINANCIAL IMPACT

Separate money actually outstanding from projected business loss. Reconcile gross amount, settlement amount, fees, tax, refunds, chargebacks, and adjustments. Label estimates clearly and retain the source data and calculation method.

REVIEW BEFORE SENDING

Generated correspondence is a draft. Replace placeholders, remove claims that are not supported by the merchant agreement or an authoritative source, minimise personal data, and obtain legal or accounting review where the stakes justify it.
