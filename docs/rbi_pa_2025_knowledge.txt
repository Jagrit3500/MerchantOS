RBI MASTER DIRECTION ON REGULATION OF PAYMENT AGGREGATOR (PA)
Reference: RBI/DPSS/2025-26/141 | Issued: September 15, 2025

OVERVIEW AND SCOPE

This Master Direction consolidates all RBI guidelines on Payment Aggregators (PAs). It applies to PA-Online (PA-O), PA-Physical (PA-P), and PA-Cross Border (PA-CB) entities.

KEY COMPLIANCE DEADLINE: All Payment Aggregators must complete re-KYC verification of existing merchants by September 15, 2026. Merchants not verified by this date may have payment services paused.

SECTION 2: AUTHORIZATION REQUIREMENTS

Non-bank entities must obtain authorisation from RBI under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007. Net Worth: Minimum Rs. 15 crore at application, Rs. 25 crore by end of financial year 3.

SECTION 3: ESCROW AND SETTLEMENT TIMELINES

PAs must maintain a nodal or escrow account. Settlement: T+1 for domestic, up to T+7 for international/cross-border due to correspondent banking. Holding funds beyond settlement periods without written communication is a violation.

SECTION 4: MERCHANT DUE DILIGENCE AND KYC REQUIREMENTS

Para 4.2 - MANDATORY KYC FOR ALL MERCHANTS: (1) Valid identity proof of the business entity and promoters/directors. (2) Valid address proof. (3) Proof of business existence. (4) Bank account in the name of the business entity or proprietor.

Para 4.3 - SOLE PROPRIETORSHIP:
Para 4.3(i) - Registered Sole Proprietors: Must provide a government-issued registration certificate such as GST Certificate, Udyam Registration Certificate, IEC Code, or Shop and Establishment Certificate, along with PAN and address proof.

Para 4.3(ii) - Unregistered Sole Proprietors: GSTIN (GST Certificate) is NOT mandatory. An unregistered sole proprietor may provide ANY ONE of the following as business proof: (a) Bank statement of the last 6 months, (b) Udyam Registration Certificate, (c) IEC - Importer Exporter Code, (d) MSME certificate. PAN Card and Aadhaar Card are mandatory.

NOTE FOR PAYMENT AGGREGATORS: Demanding GST Certificate from unregistered sole proprietors when the merchant can provide alternative business proof as listed in Para 4.3(ii) is NOT in compliance with these directions. A PA cannot make GST mandatory for this category of merchant.

Para 4.4 - PARTNERSHIP FIRMS: Mandatory: Firm PAN Card, Partnership Deed, PAN and Aadhaar of all partners, bank account in firm name. Any one of: GST Certificate, Bank Statement (6 months).

Para 4.5 - PRIVATE LIMITED AND PUBLIC LIMITED COMPANIES: Mandatory: Certificate of Incorporation, Company PAN Card, MOA and AOA, Board Resolution, Director PAN and Aadhaar. For Public Ltd: List of beneficial owners above 10%. Any one of: GST Certificate or audited financials.

Para 4.6 - NGO / TRUST / SOCIETY: Mandatory: Trust Deed or Society Registration Certificate, PAN Card, Trustee PAN and Aadhaar. Any one of: 12A/80G certificate, FCRA certificate for foreign donations. GST Certificate is generally NOT required for NGOs that are exempt from GST.

Para 4.7 - IN-PERSON VERIFICATION (IPV): PAs must conduct IPV of merchants using their own employees. Cannot be outsourced to third-party agencies. Video-based Customer Identification Process (V-CIP) is permitted where applicable.

Para 4.8 - CENTRAL KYC REGISTRY (CKYCR): PAs must check the Central KYC Registry for existing records before requesting fresh documents from merchants.

SECTION 5: RISK MANAGEMENT AND SETTLEMENT HOLDS

Para 5.1 - PERMITTED REASONS FOR HOLDING SETTLEMENTS: (a) Pending KYC completion. (b) Elevated chargeback or dispute ratio triggering Card Network monitoring programs (e.g. Visa VDMP at 0.9% or Mastercard ECP at 1.0%) or aggregator risk thresholds. (c) Unusual transaction velocity or sudden volume spikes. (d) Regulatory or law enforcement agency (LEA) directive. (e) Suspected fraudulent activity with documented evidence.

Para 5.2 - MANDATORY COMMUNICATION ON HOLDS: When holding funds, the PA MUST: (1) Inform the merchant in writing within 24 hours. (2) State the specific reason for the hold. (3) Provide a list of actions required to resolve the hold. (4) Provide an estimated resolution timeline. Failure to communicate is itself a violation of these directions.

Para 5.3 - CHARGEBACK MONITORING & CARD NETWORK PROGRAMS: Chargeback ratio thresholds are established primarily by Card Networks (such as Visa Dispute Monitoring Program - VDMP at 0.9% / 100 disputes, and Mastercard Excessive Chargeback Program - ECP at 1.0%). While RBI Master Directions require PAs to establish robust risk management and fraud prevention frameworks, PAs monitor merchant chargeback trends against card network rules. When settlements are held for dispute volume, merchants are entitled to provide proof of delivery, fulfillment logs, and customer communication to substantiate legitimate sales and request exclusion of isolated fraud from aggregate ratios.

Para 5.4 - RESOLUTION TIMELINES (TAT): KYC holds: Must be resolved within 7 business days of merchant submitting complete documents. Risk holds: Initial review within 48 hours, full resolution within 15 business days. LEA holds: Subject to the terms of the competent authority order.

SECTION 6: MERCHANT RIGHTS AND GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL

Para 6.1 - MERCHANT RIGHTS: Merchants have the right to: (a) Written communication stating the reason for any account hold. (b) A clear list of documents required and the legal basis for each requirement. (c) Resolution within prescribed TAT timelines. (d) Escalation to the Grievance Officer if not resolved.

Para 6.2 - GRIEVANCE OFFICER: Every PA must appoint a Grievance Officer. Name, contact details, and email must be displayed on the PA website. Grievance Officer must respond within 30 days of receiving a complaint.

Para 6.3 - RBI INTEGRATED OMBUDSMAN (PROCEDURAL JURISDICTION): If a grievance is not resolved by the Payment Aggregator within 30 days, the merchant may escalate to the RBI Integrated Ombudsman at https://cms.rbi.org.in. IMPORTANT JURISDICTIONAL LIMITATION: The Ombudsman Scheme excludes disputes that challenge a regulated entity's internal commercial risk judgment or underwriting appetite. However, complaints based on PROCEDURAL VIOLATIONS (such as failure to communicate reasons for fund hold in writing within 24 hours, withholding settlements beyond statutory TAT without written notice, or refusing to accept statutory KYC alternatives specified in Para 4.3) are fully maintainable.

SECTION 7: PROHIBITED PRACTICES

PAs CANNOT: (a) Demand documents not specified in these directions without written justification. (b) Hold settlements beyond TAT without written communication. (c) Charge merchants separately for KYC verification. (d) Refuse to provide the reason for an account hold in writing. (e) Block an account permanently without due process. (f) Make GST Certificate mandatory for all merchants irrespective of their registration status.

IMPORTANT DATES: September 15, 2025: Directions issued. January 1, 2026: New merchants must be KYC-verified before onboarding. September 15, 2026: All existing merchants must complete re-KYC. After September 15, 2026: Non-compliant merchants may have payment services paused.
