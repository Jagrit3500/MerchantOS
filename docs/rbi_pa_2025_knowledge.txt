RBI PAYMENT AGGREGATOR DIRECTIONS 2025 - VERIFIED EXCERPT GUIDE

SOURCE: Reserve Bank of India (Regulation of Payment Aggregators) Directions, 2025
REFERENCE: RBI/DPSS/2025-26/141, issued 15 September 2025
OFFICIAL URL: https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12896
LAST VERIFIED: 12 September 2026

SCOPE AND CAUTION

The Directions regulate payment aggregators. They do not create a complete merchant remedies code, a universal merchant document checklist, or a fixed settlement-hold resolution deadline. MerchantOS uses these provisions as source context and does not replace the merchant agreement, the provider's current policy, the RBI KYC Direction, tax advice, or legal advice.

DISPUTE MANAGEMENT - PARAGRAPH 8

A payment aggregator must maintain a mechanism for payment-related disputes and refund timelines. Agreements among the payment aggregator, merchants, acquiring banks, and other stakeholders must clearly allocate responsibilities for refunds, failed transactions, grievance redressal, and reconciliation. The payment aggregator must disclose its merchant policies, privacy policy, and other terms. It must appoint an officer for merchant issues and prominently publish the escalation matrix.

The Directions do not prescribe a universal five-day or thirty-day deadline for every merchant complaint. Use the provider's published escalation matrix and preserve the original complaint and response.

SECURITY AND RISK - PARAGRAPH 9

Payment aggregators must maintain risk-management, fraud-detection, and information-security controls. Paragraph 9 does not state a merchant evidence checklist or establish that any particular signal caused an account restriction. If a provider identifies a risk review, ask it to specify the trigger and remediation criteria, then submit relevant transaction records. The Directions do not publish fixed Visa or Mastercard chargeback thresholds, and MerchantOS must not represent a card-network threshold as an RBI threshold.

MERCHANT DUE DILIGENCE - PARAGRAPH 13

A payment aggregator must conduct customer due diligence of merchants under the RBI Master Direction - Know Your Customer. With merchant consent, it should retrieve the merchant's KYC record from CKYCR. If no current CKYCR record is available, other KYC mechanisms may be used.

For a merchant whose annual turnover does not exceed Rs 40 lakh, or annual export turnover does not exceed Rs 5 lakh, paragraph 13(b) permits an alternative process: verify PAN or Form 60, conduct Contact Point Verification, and obtain and verify the applicable officially valid document or equivalent e-document. Eligibility and the exact document depend on the merchant and the KYC Direction.

The PA Directions do not contain the previously attributed paragraphs 4.3(i), 4.3(ii), 4.4, 4.5, or 4.6 setting separate GST and document lists for every merchant type. A provider may still request GST or other business evidence under its current onboarding policy, the merchant's risk profile, or other applicable law. Ask the provider to identify the basis and acceptable alternatives.

ASSISTED DUE DILIGENCE - PARAGRAPH 15

A non-bank payment aggregator may use authorised agents for specified Digital KYC and assisted V-CIP activities. The payment aggregator retains ultimate responsibility for due diligence. Contact Point Verification is not the same as an RBI-mandated in-person interview for every merchant.

SETTLEMENT AND ESCROW - PARAGRAPH 16 AND TABLE 1

A non-bank payment aggregator must keep merchant collections in the prescribed escrow account. The credit timeline to a merchant's account is governed by the agreement between the payment aggregator and merchant. That agreement must be fair and equitable and transparently state settlement timelines.

The 2025 Directions do not impose a universal statutory T+2 settlement cycle. MerchantOS compares a report against an operator-configured expectation. The provider's dashboard and merchant agreement control the applicable schedule.

TRANSITION DATES

Merchants onboarded through 31 December 2025 must be brought into compliance with paragraph 13 within one year from 15 September 2025. From 1 January 2026, new merchants should be onboarded under the due-diligence requirements in the Directions.

WHAT TO REQUEST FROM A PROVIDER

Ask for the dashboard status, the reason for the restriction, the specific remediation items, the applicable merchant-policy or agreement clause, the expected review or settlement date, the merchant-grievance contact, and the escalation matrix. Phrase these as requests unless a verified source creates a specific entitlement.
